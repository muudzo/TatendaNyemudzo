## Context

People who report land-rights abuses in Zimbabwe are often watched. The people they report on can
compel data from hosting providers, seize devices, and watch network traffic. The app is for
citizens, activists and NGOs organising around illegal land deals.

## Decisions

I started with a threat model rather than a feature list: who the adversaries are, what they can
do, and, just as important, **what this app cannot protect against**.

<Note kind="decision">
  The UI is forbidden from saying "military-grade encryption". The allowed wording is written
  down: *"Reports are encrypted in your browser with AES-GCM-256 before sending."*
</Note>

Honest copy is a safety feature. Someone who overestimates their protection takes risks they
wouldn't otherwise take. The residual risks are listed for users too: network timing, a
compromised device, photo metadata, legal compulsion.

## Build

- **Real cryptography.** The first version had "simulated AES". I replaced it with WebCrypto
  AES-GCM-256 using PBKDF2 (250,000 iterations).
- **Exactly-once reporting.** Submissions carry idempotency keys, retry with a circuit breaker, and
  queue offline, so a flaky connection never loses a report or sends it twice.
- **Emergency wipe.** One control clears local storage, IndexedDB, caches and the service worker.
- **Nothing sensitive cached.** The service worker never caches `/api/`, and the error boundary
  never logs report contents.
- **A small bundle.** A 300 KB gzip budget is enforced in CI, because on metered data size
  decides who can use the app at all.

## Where it stands

Live as a PWA with CI security scanning (CodeQL, npm audit, gitleaks). The biggest open item is
removing photo metadata on the device before upload, because EXIF data can identify a reporter
even when everything else is anonymous.
