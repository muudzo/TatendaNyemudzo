## Context

In Zimbabwe, cattle are a savings account, traded at physical auctions: one place, one day a week.
Paynow, the country's largest payment gateway, gave me six weeks to build a livestock marketplace
and use it to benchmark Paynow's developer experience against its competitors.

## Problem

Online livestock trade already happens on Facebook and WhatsApp. It's cheap, and it has no escrow,
no verification and plenty of no-shows. The auction house is trustworthy but expensive. The real
question: **what does the auction house actually sell, and can a phone provide it for less?**

## Thinking

I went to two auctions before designing anything.

<Note kind="evidence">
  A **US$1,000 deposit** to bid, where average monthly income is around US$200–300. And a **12%
  house fee** on every sale.
</Note>

The deposit keeps out every smallholder who wants one goat. The room is full on a Wednesday, so
anyone with a job, anyone rural and the diaspora are all missing. That became the wedge.

<SignaturePlate slug="zimlivestock" />

The highest bid card was **45**, in a room of about 200. A marketplace doesn't need the room. It
needs about 45 serious bidders per listing.

<Note kind="decision">
  Don't compete with Facebook on price. Offer **auction-level trust at half the fee**.
</Note>

## Design

| The auction house | ZimLivestock |
|---|---|
| US$1,000 deposit | US$50–100 refundable escrow |
| 12% in fees | 5–6%, shown line by line at checkout |
| Physical inspection | Video, stock-card photos, seller history |
| A full room | Live bids and a "people watching" count |
| Wednesday, one place | Any day, anywhere with a phone |

## Build

A React PWA on Supabase. The parts that matter are invisible:

- **Bids have one door**: a database function that locks the listing and checks every rule.
- **Exactly once**: idempotency keys on bids and payments, so a double-tap never pays twice.
- **Production polices itself**: every merge runs security, consistency and chaos tests against
  the live app.

To benchmark fairly, I integrated all five payment providers into the same checkout.

## Iteration

On 13 April, the production checks found that **anyone could win any auction for $1**: a loose
database policy let users write bids directly and skip the rules. I closed it the same day, along
with two other severity-1 issues.

<Note kind="evidence">`bf3da08` · `921bc62` · `4cd7109`</Note>

My own tests had passed. The system attacking production is what caught it.

## Result

- A live marketplace: list, bid, win, pay.
- A ranked developer-experience report with seven recommendations. Stripe scored 9.7/10, Paynow 4.2.
- The key finding: Paynow's API sits behind bot protection that blocks **all** server-side calls.
  Until that changes, it can't be used from modern cloud apps.

<Note kind="open">
  12 of 15 success criteria green at handover. Live payments blocked, user feedback about 30%
  collected.
</Note>

## Reflection

One visit to an auction gave me the deposit, the fee, the wedge and the target. I built the
safety net early and user feedback late. Next time, the first user session happens the same week
as the first deploy.
