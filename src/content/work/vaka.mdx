## Context

*Vaka* is Shona for "to build". In Zimbabwe, the local council inspects every new building five
times, from foundation to final sign-off, and charges a fee at each stage. Each inspection means
booking an inspector, waiting, getting a paper card signed and paying, often in cash.

## Problem

Paper gets lost, cash leaks before it reaches the council's books, and a single inspector covers
several offices. Underneath all three: **the order of stages is only a convention.** Nothing stops
a slab going down before the foundation passes.

## Thinking

I started with the by-laws and council guidance, and marked every figure I couldn't confirm as
`[validate]`.

<Note kind="evidence">
  Harare and Bulawayo already accept Paynow for council bills.
</Note>

That changed the product. Digital payment wasn't the innovation. What no council has is the
workflow in between: stage gating, inspector sign-off, a reconciled audit trail.

<Note kind="decision">
  Vaka is the **workflow layer** on the payment rail councils already trust. It never holds money.
</Note>

## Design

One rule: a stage unlocks only after the previous stage is **paid** and **passed** on site.

<SignaturePlate slug="vaka" />

The same engine serves four people:

| Persona | What they need |
|---|---|
| Applicant | One clear next action |
| Inspector | Today's jobs; photo, GPS, pass or fail |
| Council | Fees reconciled, a full audit trail |
| Ministry | The national picture |

Finished buildings get a QR certificate anyone can verify online. A paper certificate is easy to
forge; a record in the registry isn't.

## Build

<Note kind="decision">
  Self-hosted NestJS and Postgres, not a hosted backend. Government data has to stay in the country.
</Note>

- Role checks on every endpoint, and an **append-only audit entry** for every payment and status
  change.
- Inspection and stage unlock commit together, or not at all.
- Payments run server-side only, with a simulation mode so the demo needs no keys.

## Iteration

The first plan digitised everything: plan approval, many councils, contractor lookups. I cut it
to one loop, **pay → inspect → unlock**, where the pain and the return are sharpest.

## Result

A working prototype with a three-minute demo: the applicant pays, the inspector passes the stage,
the next one unlocks, and the council sees the fee already reconciled.

<Note kind="open">
  No council has used it yet. The next step is a free pilot with one council.
</Note>

## Reflection

The most valuable decision here was about positioning, not a screen. And I designed the rule
first, which is what ZimLivestock taught me. Next time I'll talk to an inspector before designing
the inspector's app.
